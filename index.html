The inversion

Capability is not a scaling problem.

Every frontier model is a bank of learned computation. How you organize and reuse that computation — not how much of it you buy — is the lever almost no one is pulling.

The industry's bet

Capability = scale

Train a bigger model. Add more GPUs. Build another datacenter. Spend to move the frontier up — and pay again at the next model.

Our proof

Capability = organization

Get more from a fixed substrate by acquiring reusable structure over it. No new parameters. No retraining. The frontier moves sideways.

We don't make the model bigger. We make it do more.

The primitive

A reusable interface over a frozen substrate.

Don't run the system's intended program. Recombine its own fixed parts — steered by a small, reusable interface that forecasts which parts to compose — and let a ground-truth oracle that can't be gamed decide what's real.

The advantage lives in the acquired interface, not in the model. So the engine is model-agnostic and future-proof: every new frontier release is simply more latent capability to harness, at no retraining cost. The interfaces compound like a codebase — they appreciate as substrates improve, instead of depreciating the moment the next model ships.
